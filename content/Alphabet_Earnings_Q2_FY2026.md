The guidance picture is one of rising commitment against falling specificity. Every number that governs spending was raised or reaffirmed upward; every number that governs return — 2027 capex, Cloud margin, TPU economics, FCF recovery — became more qualified. That asymmetry is the quarter’s clearest signal about management’s own visibility.

**Accounting and non-recurring clarifications**

• A $2.1B charge for the Stockholm Patent and Market Court decision in the PriceRunner (Klarna) action: $1.5B of principal damages in Google Services G&A and $581M of interest and costs in OI&E. Google Services operating margin was 41.8% reported, approximately 43.4% excluding the charge.

• OI&E of $98.0B included $99.0B of net gains on equity securities, primarily unrealised marks on SpaceX and one private company — equivalent to roughly $8.04 per diluted share before tax, against reported diluted EPS of $9.11. Reported EPS and net income are not a read on operating performance this quarter; operating income of $40.8B is.

• A $7.7B inventory build in H1 2026, principally TPU systems held for sale, is a use of operating cash that precedes the associated revenue by roughly a year.

• Q2 2026 revenue carried an approximately one-point FX tailwind; Q3 is guided to a slight headwind.

_→ Underlying economic performance: strong and improving at the operating line, materially weaker at the cash line — and the gap between the two is the investment case._

Over a five-to-ten year horizon the question is no longer whether Alphabet keeps its position, but what return on capital that position now requires. A business that converted roughly 44% of operating cash flow into free cash flow in FY2025 converts under 30% today. If AI compute earns what the last three years of compute earned, this is the cheapest moat purchase in the company’s history; if it earns half, Alphabet has bought a lower-return version of the business it already owned.

**Buybacks, issuance and dilution**

• Buybacks: $11.5B (Q3 2025), $5.5B (Q4 2025), $0 (Q1 2026), $0 (Q2 2026). The programme is suspended, not tapered.

• Issuance: $49.6B of common and 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred, plus a $40.0B ATM authorisation (undrawn at June 30) to fund SBC tax obligations.

• Debt: $20.0B of US-dollar and $31.8B of foreign-currency senior notes issued in 2026; long-term debt $46.5B → $98.2B in six months, against roughly $16B twelve months ago.

• Net effect on share count: diluted shares 12,198M → 12,309M y/y, a 0.9% increase, before the mandatory preferred converts. Preferred dividends now appear in the EPS bridge ($86M in Q2).

• Acquisitions: $33.7B in H1 2026 — Wiz ($29.5B, into Cloud) and Intersect ($5.9B, data-centre energy). Goodwill $33.4B → $57.8B; intangibles $1.3B → $9.1B.

• Dividend: raised 5% to $0.22 per quarter — the one shareholder return still growing, and the smallest.

**Long-term compounding implications**

For a long-term owner the arithmetic has inverted. Through FY2025 Alphabet returned capital while growing; in H1 2026 it raised capital while growing, and per-share economics moved against the holder for the first time in a decade of buybacks. Whether that is good capital allocation depends entirely on the return earned on $195–205B of 2026 capex and a larger 2027 number — a return management describes qualitatively and has never quantified. Free cash flow per share of $4.33 on a trailing basis, against a share price in the $330s, means the market is paying for deferred earnings, not for current cash. That is a defensible position, but it is a position, not a fact.

**INVESTOR QUESTIONS — WHAT A LONG-TERM OWNER MUST NOW REASSESS**

|**Question**|**Why it matters**|
|---|---|
|What return does management actually underwrite on the $195–205B of 2026 capex, and over what payback period?|Three calls have invoked a “disciplined ROIC framework” without a single figure. If the hurdle is materially below the ~30% returns Alphabet has historically earned on capital, the whole programme is value-dilutive even if it is revenue-accretive.|
|Why did purchase commitments rise $478.6B in a quarter when Cloud backlog rose $52B?|If the commitments are chips, land and power bought ahead of contracted demand, Alphabet has taken merchant risk on AI compute for the first time. That is a different business than renting capacity against signed contracts.|
|Is the cost-per-click deceleration a mix effect or a value effect?|If AI-driven incremental queries are structurally worth less per click, Search revenue growth becomes dependent on perpetual volume expansion — a far more fragile engine than the price-and-volume compounding of the last decade.|
|What is Cloud’s operating margin excluding TPU system sales, Wiz, and third-party bridging capacity?|Management says growth accelerated ex-TPU but has never quantified either growth or margin ex-hardware. Without it, the 35.6% figure cannot be used to underwrite anything.|
|How concentrated is the $513.9B backlog among counterparties Alphabet also funds or backstops?|Revenue booked from customers whose data centres Alphabet backstops and whose equity Alphabet holds is lower quality than the headline implies, and correlates on the way down.|
|Why was the 350M paid-subscription disclosure dropped, and will Gemini Enterprise paid seats return?|Subscriptions are the one consumer line where Alphabet charges directly for AI. If disclosure is being withdrawn as growth slows, the consumer AI monetisation story is weaker than the product narrative suggests.|
|What would cause management to reduce capex rather than raise it?|No downside condition has ever been articulated. An investment programme with no stated stopping rule is a commitment, not an allocation decision.|
|Does the six-year server life survive another generation of accelerators?|With depreciation up 42% y/y and the 10-Q newly flagging that technology advances could change the benefit period, a life extension would flatter reported margins precisely when underlying economics are deteriorating.|
